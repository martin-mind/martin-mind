At Autodesk, scale is the challenge. With multiple product teams and a complex component ecosystem, the real work is creating a shared language — not just building UI, but defining how decisions get made and communicated.

Design system in context — components structured for multi-team adoption and product consistency

Benchmark & audit — mapping competitor patterns and internal inconsistencies to guide component strategy

Context & Role

Bringing structure to a complex, multi-team design ecosystem

Autodesk's product surface spans multiple enterprise tools used by millions of designers, engineers, and architects worldwide. The design org faced a growing problem: components were inconsistent across products, decisions were siloed, and there was no clear strategy for how UI patterns should evolve.

My role was to define and drive the UX strategy for component standardisation — from auditing the existing library and benchmarking industry patterns, to co-defining components with engineering and aligning multiple product teams around a shared system.

Research & Insights

Understanding where the system was breaking

The audit revealed that over 60% of UI components existed in multiple variants across different product teams with no shared token base. This fragmentation created inconsistent experiences for end users and significant design debt for teams trying to ship fast.

Through stakeholder interviews and design reviews, I identified three core friction points: naming inconsistency, unclear component ownership, and absent documentation for complex interaction states.

Strategy
A
Token-first approachEstablish shared design tokens for color, spacing, typography and elevation before defining component anatomy
B
Atomic component modelDefine components at the most granular level, ensuring composability and flexibility across diverse product contexts
C
Cross-team alignment ritualsWeekly design system office hours and async review channels to build shared ownership and surface conflicts early
Impact & Outcomes

40+ components documented with full anatomy, states, variants and accessibility specs

Reduced design inconsistency across 3 major product surfaces

Shared token architecture adopted as foundation for all future component work

Design-to-engineering handoff time reduced through clear component contracts

Established a scalable governance model for the design system org
